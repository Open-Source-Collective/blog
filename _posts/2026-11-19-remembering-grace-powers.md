It is with a heavy heart that we share the news that our beloved co-worker and friend, Grace Powers passed away on October 30th after a short but fierce battle with cancer.

If you've worked with open source funding in the last few years, you've likely had a question about a payout, an invoice, or a compliance form and have run into Grace. You might not have known her name, but she was the friendly face in our onboarding videos, welcoming every new project we hosted. You could count on things working because of her. She ensured our 2,700 open source member projects received the support they needed. Grace made sure every question was answered and every maintainer’s issue that prevented them from receiving funding was resolved. She championed open source and connected people to build a stronger, more resilient community. This wasn’t because it was her job; this was who Grace was.

Grace grew up in Toledo, Ohio, and started her career as a patient advocate at a health clinic. She earned her bachelor's and master's degrees in social work at the University of Toledo, where she learned to help people in crisis and fix the systems around them. She brought that to every job she held, from health and patient advocacy to tech. Grace started her career in tech with Pocket, then part of Mozilla. It was here that Grace learned about open source and brought her skills of empathy and connection. Her colleagues at Pocket remembered the signed birthday cards she mailed to remote employees and the books she collected for every new baby. When the pandemic hit, she held listening sessions with more than 40 coworkers to check on how they were doing.

Grace joined the Open Source Collective team in June of 2023. We were incredibly fortunate to have had the opportunity to work alongside her. Her positivity was infectious, only matched by her wit, optimism, and tenacity. She had a distinctive vision of the world that inspired those around her to dare to hope for a brighter world. She challenged us to face big problems with kindness and empathy. We’ll miss her wry, insightful reviews of indie movies and her joyful karaoke of “Rebel Girl” by Bikini Kill, feminist punk icons that inspired her youth and shaped her values. Grace’s impact on her communities was immeasurable; we will miss her every day and do our best to carry her spirit forward. In her words: “Let's gooooo!!!”

**How we're honoring her**

Grace spent her life helping people get the care they needed. She was a fierce advocate for women's health and reproductive rights - the belief that every woman should have the ability to make decisions about their own body, sexuality, and reproduction without coercion or discrimination, so we're raising money in her name for the Midwest Access Coalition. Since 2015, the coalition has helped people across the Midwest, including Ohio, travel for care, and it covers their lodging, child care, and food while they're away. <https://opencollective.com/opensource/projects/grace-powers-memorial>

Please give if you can and share the fundraiser if you can't. And please share a story about Grace on her memorial page: <https://www.mykeeper.com/GracePowers>. She knew there was a person behind every one of those 2,700 projects, and we want to remember the person she was.

We are better because she was here.
