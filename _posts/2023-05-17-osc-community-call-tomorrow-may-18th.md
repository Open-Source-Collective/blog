It's worth mentioning here that there was a great discussion last time about **README**s. Ruth Cheesley of [Mautic](https://opencollective.com/mautic) asked:

> With this change maybe coming soon to GitHub I’m thinking about how to improve our README to make it a better experience for our users and contributors. I’d love to know of any READMEs you’ve come across on your travels that you really like, and why!

Out of this came the following links, that may help people out:

-   [https://github.com/matiassingers/awesome-readme](https://github.com/matiassingers/awesome-readme) (A list of awesome readmes)
-   [https://github.com/hackergrrl/art-of-readme](https://github.com/hackergrrl/art-of-readme) (How to write good ones)
-   [https://github.com/RichardLitt/standard-readme#readme](https://github.com/RichardLitt/standard-readme#readme) (A standard for simple code packages)
-   [https://github.com/LappleApple/feedmereadmes#readme](https://github.com/LappleApple/feedmereadmes#readme) (A place to throw READMEs)
-   What I used to use to audit repos and orgs and READMEs: [https://github.com/mntnr/audit-templates/](https://github.com/mntnr/audit-templates/) 

If anyone is interested, you can also feedme,  richard@opencollective.com, with your READMEs! I am happy to review and give some feedback.
